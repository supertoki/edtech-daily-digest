# EdTech Daily Digest — September 7, 2026

## Executive Summary

The back-to-school AI conversation has shifted from policy memos to lived experience: EdSurge has a principal and a student directly reviewing OpenAI's new ChatGPT for Teens, while EdTech Digest's "Reindustrializing the American Classroom" and its companion piece on AI's "shape of the box" both argue the real fight is holding the line between **AI literacy and AI dependence**, not banning the tools outright. That tension runs straight into implementation: EdSurge's on-the-ground reporting on putting edtech guidance to work finds new rules land very differently in a pre-K classroom than in a policy document, a gap CoSN's back-to-school resource roundup tries to close with practical guidance spanning AI, cybersecurity, screen time, and procurement. Infrastructure decisions are getting more deliberate too — EdTech Magazine's new piece on hybrid cloud for K-12 walks through what should live on-premises versus in the cloud, while Ed-Fi's case for a unified "data hub" argues real-time, standards-based data can make reporting smarter and simpler at once, an interoperability push 1EdTech is extending internationally with Learning Impact Europe in Thessaloniki and its recent joint session linking education and workforce data with state workforce agencies. On the money side, Inside Higher Ed's presidents' survey finds AI now viewed as the most impactful force facing higher ed by 2030, ahead of enrollment and financial pressure, even as EdTech Magazine's own procurement guide and a fresh Education Technology Insights leadership essay both push IT and academic leaders toward more intentional, friction-reducing technology investment rather than reflexive AI spending. The fall conference calendar keeps filling in behind all of it: ISTE+ASCD's Edtech Leader Certification enrollment closes September 17, EdTech Week has confirmed its October 14-15 return to Columbia University, and Ed-Fi's own Alliance Summit is now set for November.
