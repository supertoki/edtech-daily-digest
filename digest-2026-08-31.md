# EdTech Daily Digest — August 31, 2026

## Executive Summary

The Department of Education's August 20 Dear Colleague letter is rippling into procurement practice: coverage from THE Journal, EdSurge, and MarketScale (aggregated via EdTech Digest) describes districts and vendors now expected to show **"purpose, proof, and process"** in edtech contracts, with usage metrics like log-ins no longer sufficient justification — a shift arriving as K-12 districts already juggle an average of **2,591 separate edtech tools**. Cybersecurity stays urgent and concrete: Inside Higher Ed reports the University of Texas at San Antonio delayed the start of its fall semester after an attempted breach at the network edge, while EdTech Magazine covers community colleges centralizing security operations as EDUCAUSE's 2026 list ranks collaborative cybersecurity a top higher-ed priority. On AI governance, the center of gravity keeps shifting toward students and educators themselves: EdSurge and AASA report more than 130 student leaders from all 50 states approved the **STUDENTS FIRST Act**, a first-of-its-kind student-authored national AI policy framework, while ISTE's updated standards fold AI literacy into existing digital-citizenship indicators rather than building a separate track, and Brookings publishes companion pieces on AI surveillance risks and school AI councils. In higher ed policy, Inside Higher Ed tracks the Trump administration's new accreditation overhaul and continuing fallout from Secretary McMahon's reform push, alongside research questioning how equitable **"meritocratic"** admissions really are. On the standards and conference calendar, 1EdTech's September 1 discussion with NASWA on workforce development leads into its Learning Impact Europe conference in Thessaloniki, while Ed-Fi's summer AI working group continues examining what its data standard needs for an AI-driven ecosystem.
