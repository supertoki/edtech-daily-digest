# EdTech Daily Digest — September 11, 2026

## Executive Summary

Inside Higher Ed leads with three same-week, data-driven stories that together paint a fractured picture of the sector: institutions are reporting **record enrollment turnarounds** even as national data shows humanities bachelor's degrees at their lowest point since 1991, and a new ACE survey finds senior college leaders anxious about financial pressure and public skepticism. That uncertainty runs alongside a parallel AI story: EdTech Magazine reports K-12 districts are handing routine IT help-desk tickets to **agentic AI** while its higher-ed coverage ties AI adoption directly to enrollment pressure, and ISTE+ASCD announces a Google partnership to bring AI literacy training to six million educators. But a counter-narrative keeps surfacing: Brookings argues schools should **go slow to go fast** on AI, MIT's own AI report calls for rethinking grading and social learning, and EdTech Digest's guest column urges a more intentional, human-centered approach to classroom technology, citing new federal tech-use guidance for the school year. Equity questions run underneath both threads &mdash; Brookings finds screen-time bans disproportionately discipline Black students, THE Journal covers CoSN's push for purpose-based screen-time policy over blanket limits, and EdTech Magazine explores what brain-computer interfaces could mean for students with disabilities. On security, THE Journal's warning about unsanctioned **shadow AI** use echoes into EdTech Magazine's higher-ed coverage of balancing AI innovation with secure core systems. The fall conference calendar keeps filling in: 1EdTech's Learning Impact Europe convenes in Thessaloniki next week, EdTech Week and the StartEd CEO Summit head to Columbia University in October, and Ed-Fi's Alliance Summit follows in November.
