# EdTech Daily Digest — September 23, 2026

## Executive Summary

Inside Higher Ed's newest CAO survey puts a number on a gap many have sensed all year: **seven in ten provosts now use AI weekly in their own work, but only one in ten institutions have a centralized AI strategy** to govern it, even as the Education Department redirects **$235 million** in minority-serving-institution funds -- $174 million to HBCUs and $61 million to tribal colleges -- and higher ed groups warn its accreditation overhaul may violate federal law. That personal-AI-outpaces-institutional-policy pattern shows up lower down the org chart too: EdTech Digest profiles a K-12 HR leader watching AI creep into teacher hiring, and EdSurge reports parents now arriving at IEP meetings armed with AI-generated research. Investment keeps climbing to match the adoption curve -- Gartner projects **worldwide AI spending will reach $2.67 trillion in 2026**, and the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative-backed Learning Commons is putting some of that money directly into K-12 edtech, opening an AI infrastructure platform that Canva Education, MagicSchool and TalkingPoints are already building on. Security keeps pace with the growth: THE Journal's coverage of help-desk-impersonation passkey phishing continues to reverberate as EdTech Magazine tracks an **11% year-over-year rise in IoT attacks**, and Brookings flags equity concerns in AI-powered school surveillance. Amid all of it, a quieter standards story keeps building: 1EdTech's 2026 trend-watch keeps pointing toward governed, interoperable ecosystems over standalone tools, Ed-Fi's impact-story library keeps making the real-time-data case, and a five-quality-indicators framework -- jointly built by 1EdTech, ISTE, CoSN, CAST, Digital Promise and SETDA -- is giving districts a shared rubric for vetting both. Fall's conference marathon is now just days out: EDUCAUSE's annual conference opens September 29, CBExchange follows September 30-October 3, ISTE has opened proposals for ISTELive 27 in Boston, and EdTech Week returns to Columbia's Teachers College October 13-15.
