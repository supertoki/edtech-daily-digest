# EdTech Daily Digest — September 10, 2026

## Executive Summary

Ed tech's AI story this week is one of contrasts. EdTech Magazine reports K-12 districts are turning to **agentic AI** to autonomously triage routine IT help-desk tickets, freeing lean staff for strategic work, while EdTech Digest profiles a new AI speaking-coach app and argues for a more intentional, human-centered approach to classroom technology. But EdSurge finds teachers pushing back as AI-generated videos aimed at preschoolers distort early learning, and Brookings makes the case for schools to **go slow to go fast** on AI given low family trust and real risks to skill development. Equity questions keep surfacing alongside: Brookings finds blanket phone bans like Florida's disproportionately discipline Black students, echoing THE Journal's earlier coverage of federal guidance on edtech and screen time, while EdSurge explores what Indigenous communities risk losing as AI promises to archive sacred knowledge. On the security front, EdTech Magazine reports district IT teams are shifting from ransomware firefighting to longer-term cyber-resilience planning, building on THE Journal's warning that unsanctioned **shadow AI** use by students and staff is becoming K-12's biggest emerging risk. In higher ed, Inside Higher Ed reports new federal data showing 53% of college students have taken an online course since 2022 (up from 36% in 2019), the same week EdTech Magazine's read of the EDUCAUSE Horizon Report finds AI and enrollment pressure jointly reshaping campus teaching and operations. The fall conference calendar keeps filling in behind it all: 1EdTech hosts a Spanish-language assessment webinar today ahead of its Learning Impact Europe conference in Thessaloniki, THE Journal's Tech Tactics in Education goes virtual September 23, and Ed-Fi's Alliance Summit and EdTech Week's Columbia gathering round out the season.
