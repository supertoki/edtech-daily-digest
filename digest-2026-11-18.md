# EdTech Daily Digest — November 18, 2026

## Executive Summary

Wednesday's digest leads with **student aid and federal pressure**. A Lumina Foundation and Mathematica report covered by Inside Higher Ed finds roughly **960,000 students lost federal aid in 2025** after failing satisfactory-academic-progress checks, and argues most colleges are not using the flexibility the law allows; meanwhile seven college presidents told journalists their priorities are stabilizing research funding and easing international-enrollment limits, days after a federal judge vacated the administration's student-visa duration rule. On the K-12 side, an EdSurge study of teachers using chatbots shows why **teacher judgment** still matters when AI suggests Minecraft for a lesson on circles. Security remains a drumbeat: THE Journal tracks a **41% surge in spear-phishing**, an 84% rate of AI-related incidents and new just-in-time access controls for AI agents, and hosts an education e-mail security webcast today. On the data front, 1EdTech's Member Assembly opens today in North Charleston and Ed-Fi points to statewide attendance dashboards in South Carolina, while EDUCAUSE's annual conference begins in Orlando.
