# EdTech Daily Digest — September 16, 2026

## Executive Summary

EdTech Magazine introduces a **Five Quality Indicators** framework -- built with Digital Promise, 1EdTech, CAST, CoSN, ISTE and SETDA -- giving K-12 buyers a shared rubric for vetting AI purchases, just as EdTech Digest keeps tracking new AI-native tools like Encyclo-AI's research-and-curation assistant and argues edtech's next phase is about stewardship rather than speed. Brookings reinforces that theme from two angles: a case for **'going slow to go fast'** on generative AI in K-12 classrooms, and a report flagging an equity blind spot in school screen-time policies for students with disabilities, both extending the ongoing Generation AI Starts Early symposium. Interoperability groups keep positioning themselves as AI's connective tissue: Ed-Fi's data-standard work and 1EdTech's 2026 trend commentary argue governed data infrastructure is a prerequisite for trustworthy AI, not an afterthought, while 1EdTech's own Learning Impact Europe conference runs its second day in Thessaloniki. Higher ed policy stayed contentious around international students -- a new GOP bill would cap international athletes at 20% of a varsity roster, and a Texas lieutenant governor's AI-generated political ad drew on three public universities' branding without clear permission, extending fallout from this week's blocked DHS duration-of-stay rule. EdSurge added six journalists and educators to its advisory board to sharpen its AI-in-teaching coverage, publishing alongside a piece on the 'October Blues' that hit teachers weeks into the new school year. ISTE closed enrollment on a September 15 Edtech Leader Certification cohort as EDUCAUSE's annual conference, EdTech Week and the Ed-Fi Alliance Summit approach later this fall.
