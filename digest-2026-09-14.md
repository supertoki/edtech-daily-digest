# EdTech Daily Digest — September 14, 2026

## Executive Summary

Inside Higher Ed leads with MIT researcher Justin Reich's warning that the evidence base for AI's educational benefits is **"almost nonexistent,"** even as colleges sign multimillion-dollar AI contracts faster than independent research can evaluate them. That gap between deployment and evidence runs through the rest of today's coverage: EdTech Magazine details a new hybrid-cloud infrastructure guide for K-12 IT teams, THE Journal's archive shows Renaissance and Kiddom continuing to push all-in-one 'Education Intelligence' and AI-instruction platforms, and EdSurge reports AI-generated video content is **warping early learning** according to teachers on the ground. On governance, Education Technology Insights' CXO column argues for a **strategic, ethics-first** approach to AI adoption, while EdTech Digest's guest column on 'reindustrializing' the classroom makes a similar case for deliberate, human-centered technology use. Screen time remains a live equity issue: Brookings' new survey finds parents and teens broadly support school cellphone bans and don't perceive major downsides a year in, echoing THE Journal's coverage of CoSN's push for purpose-based screen-time policy over blanket limits. Equity questions extend further as EdSurge reports parents are bringing AI tools into IEP meetings and EdTech Magazine explores what brain-computer interfaces could mean for students with disabilities. The fall conference calendar is about to open in earnest: 1EdTech's Learning Impact Europe convenes in Thessaloniki starting tomorrow, ISTE+ASCD's Edtech Leader Certification cohort enrollment closes September 17, and EdTech Week, the StartEd CEO Summit and Ed-Fi's Alliance Summit follow in October and November.
