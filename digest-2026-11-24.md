# EdTech Daily Digest — November 24, 2026

## Executive Summary

Tuesday's digest centers on **trust**. Inside Higher Ed reports that most faculty now use AI in some form, but with **no shared line between AI-assisted writing and misconduct**, fear is discouraging disclosure in what it calls an AI 'witch hunt.' A companion opinion piece asks what colleges' claims of **'AI leadership'** really mean. On the K-12 side, an EdTech Digest column built on interviews with twelve district leaders finds they now **weigh vendor trust above price or features**, with some moving to outcomes-based contracts. Federal policy also moved: Inside Higher Ed lays out **where the international student crackdown stands** after court losses, with a proposed OPT fee as the next fight. A Philadelphia Fed study suggests **new graduate loan caps are unlikely to reduce defaults**, and NSF data show first-time science and health grad enrollment fell about 10%. On standards, 1EdTech's Learning Impact Award nominations have closed, with finalists to be notified by Nov. 30, and the **QTI survey closes the same day**.

## Key Themes & Trends

**1. AI, Writing & Academic Trust.** Faculty use AI widely but lack **shared rules on disclosure**, and detector false flags are changing how both students and professors write.

**2. AI in K-12 Classrooms.** Districts are setting **grade-banded AI expectations** and pushing back on Google's AI-by-default search for student accounts.

**3. Trust, Evidence & EdTech Buying.** District leaders now rank **vendor trust above features**, and evidence requirements and screen-time rules are reshaping purchases.

**4. Federal Policy & Enrollment.** Court rulings on **international students**, new graduate loan caps and a **10% drop in new STEM-health grad students** are the main federal pressures on colleges.

**5. Cybersecurity & Governance.** With **half of K-12 schools** reporting a cyber incident and phishing growing more targeted, governance lags behind the threat.

**6. Interoperability & Standards.** The **QTI survey**, Blackboard's **Dec. 31 LTI 1.3 deadline** and the Ed-Fi API v8.1 rollout set the year-end standards agenda.
