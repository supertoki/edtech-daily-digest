# EdTech Daily Digest — November 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

The day's biggest K-12 story is Google's plan to switch off its AI tools **and Search** by default for student accounts in early 2027: because AI is now built into Search, blocking one means losing the other. Large districts including New York City, Los Angeles and Chicago are reportedly weighing other search engines, and filtering vendors are adding AI Overview blocks. That fits a wider push on **AI feature creep**, with EdTech Magazine urging districts to inventory and risk-tier AI features that arrive inside tools they already approved. In higher ed, law school acceptance rates fell to a **20-year low** as applications jumped 18%. 1EdTech's Learning Impact Award nominations close **Nov. 23**, and Ed-Fi's API v8.1 and 2027 roadmap continue to set the data-standards agenda.

## Key Themes & Trends

**1. Big Tech AI Defaults in Schools.** Google's plan to make **AI and Search off by default** for students is forcing districts to choose between AI exposure and losing search altogether.

**2. AI in the Classroom.** Teachers and districts are working out how to keep **student thinking central**, from grade-banded AI expectations to AI-free writing courses.

**3. AI Governance & Vendor Accountability.** Districts are building **AI tool inventories and risk tiers**, and want vendors to give notice before adding AI to approved products.

**4. Evidence & Outcome-Led Buying.** Buyers are moving from procurement checklists to **outcome-led design**, backed by federal encouragement to weigh effectiveness evidence.

**5. Interoperability & Data Standards.** Ed-Fi's **API v8.1** and 2027 roadmap, plus 1EdTech's QTI survey and LTI work, are shaping the next wave of data plumbing.

**6. Admissions & Access.** Law school acceptance rates hit a **20-year low** as applications surged, and widening admission gaps are back in focus.
