# EdTech Daily Digest — September 24, 2026

## Executive Summary

The week's sharpest irony comes out of Dartmouth, where student journalists ran the provost's published writing through the AI detector Pangram and found a **median AI-written score of 96%** -- including a Washington Post column in which Provost Santiago Schnell urged colleges to move beyond simply catching students who cheat with AI. It lands the same week NYC's nation-leading K-12 generative-AI moratorium keeps drawing fire: EdTech Digest argues the ban **through eighth grade** won't work, even as Brookings makes the case for schools to **"go slow to go fast"** on AI adoption instead. Higher ed's alternative-pathways debate got louder too, as Andreessen Horowitz opened applications for its **$42 million** unaccredited Horowitz Andreessen Academy, an AI-era college alternative backed by Anthropic, Google, Meta, Nvidia, Palantir and OpenAI. Regulators are moving in parallel -- California Governor Newsom's new executive order pushes frontier AI labs toward **independent, onsite verification** and an AI "kill switch," with recommended legal changes due November 16. On the operations side, EdTech Magazine flags the IT-budgeting fallout of the AI boom: K-12 districts weighing what workloads belong in the cloud versus on-prem, and university IT leaders bracing for **volatile, consumption-based AI pricing**. And fall's conference marathon is now just days out -- EDUCAUSE's annual conference opens September 29, CBExchange follows September 30-October 3, and EdTech Week returns to Columbia's Teachers College October 13-15.
