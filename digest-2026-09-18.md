# EdTech Daily Digest — September 18, 2026

## Executive Summary

Writing instruction is being pulled in opposite directions at either end of the pipeline: an Inside Higher Ed opinion piece argues that AI has made the take-home essay untenable and is quietly hollowing out humanities pedagogy, the same week EdTech Digest covers Discovery Education's expansion of its **Mystery Writing** program into kindergarten and first grade to build foundational writing skills before AI ever enters the picture. Higher ed policy pressure keeps broadening beyond the classroom: Scholars at Risk's **Free to Think 2026** report documents 382 attacks on academic freedom across 59 countries in the past year -- including at least 40 in the U.S. -- while a Senate proposal to remake college sports draws NCAA support and a Texas professor's 16-month unexplained suspension adds to the same week's fallout from international-student fights and a university-branding flap. On funding, EdTech Digest's investor roundup shows venture capital continuing to chase workplace learning over K-12 -- European learning-and-work funding more than doubled from 2024 to 2025 and stayed strong into 2026 -- underscoring a broader edtech-VC shift toward AI tools and workforce training with clearer returns. Brookings revisits school cellphone bans a year in, finding phone use at school actually rose despite near-universal bans, with the smallest increases at the strictest schools and little reported effect on student wellbeing either way. Conference season pivots from Europe to the U.S.: 1EdTech's Learning Impact Europe wrapped its three-day run in Thessaloniki, EdTech Week's full October agenda at Columbia's Teachers College is now live, and EDUCAUSE's annual conference opens September 29 -- all while EdTech Magazine's new Five Quality Indicators procurement framework and Ed-Fi's data-standard work keep pressing interoperability and vetting as prerequisites for trustworthy AI adoption, not afterthoughts.
