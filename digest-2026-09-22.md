# EdTech Daily Digest — September 22, 2026

## Executive Summary

Two data points landed this week that make AI's labor-market bite concrete: Inside Higher Ed reports a new Census Bureau working paper, **Graduating into Disruption**, found the most AI-exposed college majors -- computer science, accounting, journalism and engineering -- saw initial employment odds fall five percentage points and full-quarter earnings drop **13%**, losses comparable to graduating into a major recession, while nursing and education graduates were largely spared. That finding lands the same week THE Journal covers a new IBM study showing **46% of executives and 60% of employees** already see AI eroding critical skills like judgment and critical thinking, even as AI reshapes which capabilities matter most at work. Campus security keeps widening its own front: THE Journal also tracks new passkey-phishing techniques driving cloud account takeovers, EdTech Magazine finds IoT attacks up 11% year-over-year including in higher ed, and EdSurge profiles an Alabama high school building a next-generation cybersecurity classroom to meet the moment. Early learning faces pressure from a different direction -- EdSurge reports teachers pushing back on thin AI-generated videos aimed at preschoolers, even as new federal pre-K guidance, written with older students in mind, proves hard to translate into four-year-olds' classrooms. Interoperability work continues on a steady cadence: 1EdTech's LTI Project Group meets today to keep aligning the spec with OAuth 2.1 and its Edu-API Project Group follows tomorrow, while Ed-Fi's growing library of impact stories keeps making the case for governed data as AI adoption accelerates. Fall's conference marathon is now just a week out: EDUCAUSE's annual conference opens September 29, 1EdTech's CBExchange follows September 30, ISTE has opened proposals for ISTELive 27 in Boston, and EdTech Week returns to Columbia's Teachers College October 13-15.
