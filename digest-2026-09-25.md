# EdTech Daily Digest — September 25, 2026

## Executive Summary

Inside Higher Ed reports a wave of cyberattacks on British academic blogs that researchers suspect is an effort by authoritarian regimes to shut down work documenting human-rights abuses -- a reminder that campus IT threats extend well beyond ransomware. The warning lands alongside Inside Higher Ed's 2026 survey of chief academic officers, which finds **roughly seven in ten provosts** now use AI in their own work at least weekly -- mostly to summarize documents and draft communications -- yet only **one in ten** report their institution has a centralized AI strategy, with staff resistance and capacity gaps cited as the biggest barriers. On the policy side, THE Journal breaks down the new federal **Education Freedom Tax Credit**, a tax-credit scholarship mechanism its backers say could benefit **90% of students** in states that opt in. Workforce readiness is a running thread too: EdTech Digest's Cengage-sourced "Experience Gap" report finds **53% of new graduates** have skipped applying to entry-level jobs feeling underqualified, while EdSurge covers how the Reagan Institute's RISE summit and JFF's Maria Flynn are reframing what schools owe students entering an AI-reshaped labor market. Inside K-12 IT shops, EdTech Magazine describes agentic AI taking over routine help-desk tickets and makes the case for "AI bills of materials" to track what automated systems are actually doing, even as its own back-to-school checklist and a fresh EDUCAUSE student-tech report (46% of students hit a security threat last year) underline that the basics still need attention. Fall's conference marathon opens next week, with EDUCAUSE's annual conference starting September 29.
