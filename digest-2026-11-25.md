# EdTech Daily Digest — November 25, 2026

## Executive Summary

A new NBER survey covered by Inside Higher Ed finds only **23% of college students** admit using AI to draft essays, while **54% say their peers do**, adding to this week's debate about AI suspicion on campus. In K-12, EdSurge asks **what happens when AI outruns Title I schools**, and Google faces a **Nov. 30 deadline** to answer Sen. Markey about its plan to tie school Search access to its AI features. On procurement, district leaders continue to say **trust and evidence** matter more than features. Campus governance news includes Mott Community College removing its president, NYC's new university tech-transfer deals and a temporary dismissal of the government's Harvard admissions-data case. Standards work closes out the year with the **QTI survey due Nov. 30**, a **CLR 2.0 showcase on Dec. 3** and Blackboard's **Dec. 31 LTI 1.3 deadline**.

## Key Themes & Trends

**1. AI, Writing & Academic Trust.** Students say **peers misuse AI** far more than they do, and the gap is feeding suspicion and detector-driven policing on campus.

**2. AI Access in K-12.** Title I schools risk **falling behind on AI**, and districts are weighing alternatives to Google's AI-by-default Search before a **Nov. 30** deadline.

**3. Trust, Evidence & EdTech Buying.** District leaders rank **vendor trust above features**, while evidence requirements and fragmentation shape purchases.

**4. Federal Policy & Campus Governance.** International-student rules, the **Harvard admissions-data case**, leadership turnover and NYC's tech-transfer deals all affect colleges this week.

**5. Cybersecurity & Infrastructure.** With **half of K-12 schools** reporting a cyber incident, schools are focusing on targeted phishing, secure networks and standardized classroom tech.

**6. Interoperability & Standards.** The **QTI survey (Nov. 30)**, the **CLR 2.0 showcase (Dec. 3)** and the **LTI 1.3 cutover (Dec. 31)** set the year-end agenda.
