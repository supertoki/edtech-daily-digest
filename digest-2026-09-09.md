# EdTech Daily Digest — September 9, 2026

## Executive Summary

Ed tech's policy conversation sharpens this week as EdSurge reports the U.S. Department of Education has issued its long-awaited Dear Colleague Letter on edtech, urging states to tie purchasing decisions to **measurable evidence** rather than adoption metrics alone, while singling out Arkansas, Indiana, Louisiana, Michigan and Texas for performance-based contracting models. The guidance lands the same week Inside Higher Ed reports Senate Democrats objecting to a Pentagon-NIH research funding shift, and recaps a ransomware incident that delayed the start of classes at UT San Antonio — a reminder that the cyber-resilience push schools have been building toward all year remains unfinished. Equity concerns keep surfacing in parallel: Brookings warns that **AI-powered surveillance tools** in schools raise safety and racial-bias concerns even as adoption spreads, and THE Journal covers CoSN's push for screen-time policies that prioritize purpose over blanket restriction. On the classroom-AI front, Ed-Fi reframes its interoperability data standard for an AI-shaped future, EdTech Digest argues for a more intentional, human-centered approach to classroom technology, and EdTech Magazine tracks the AI data trends K-12 leaders are watching for the year ahead. EdSurge itself makes news, adding six journalists and educators — including Washington Post reporter Todd Frankel — to a new advisory board meant to sharpen its coverage of AI in schools. The fall conference calendar keeps filling in behind it all: 1EdTech heads to Thessaloniki for Learning Impact Europe, ISTE+ASCD opens a new Edtech Leader Certification cohort, and THE Journal's Tech Tactics in Education goes virtual on September 23, all ahead of EdTech Week's October return to Columbia and Ed-Fi's November Alliance Summit.
