# EdTech Daily Digest — November 26, 2026

## Executive Summary

Most sources published nothing new on Thanksgiving Day, so this edition rounds up the week. EdTech Magazine warns districts about **AI feature creep**: approved tools gaining AI functions after vetting. It recommends AI inventories, risk tiers and vendor notice before rollout. EdSurge looks at **AI gaps in Title I schools** and at continuity from early childhood into kindergarten. Google still faces a **Nov. 30 deadline** to answer Sen. Markey about tying school Search to its AI features. In higher ed, an NBER survey finds **23% of students** admit using AI to draft essays but **54% say peers do**, and new science and health graduate enrollment **fell about 10%**. Standards work continues with the **QTI survey (Nov. 30)**, the **CLR 2.0 showcase (Dec. 3)** and Blackboard's **Dec. 31 LTI 1.3 cutover**.

## Key Themes & Trends

**1. Governing AI Inside School Tools.** Districts are urged to **inventory AI features**, assign risk tiers and require vendor notice before AI is added to already-approved tools.

**2. AI Access & Equity in K-12.** Title I schools risk **falling behind on AI**, and districts weigh options before Google's **Nov. 30** Search-and-AI deadline.

**3. AI, Writing & Academic Trust.** Students say **peers misuse AI** far more than they do, which feeds suspicion and AI-detection policing on campus.

**4. Trust, Evidence & EdTech Buying.** District leaders put **vendor trust and evidence** ahead of features, and federal guidance pushes for proof that products work.

**5. Higher Ed Enrollment & Governance.** New **science and health grad enrollment fell about 10%**, alongside leadership turnover and federal pressure on campuses.

**6. Interoperability & Standards.** The **QTI survey (Nov. 30)**, the **CLR 2.0 showcase (Dec. 3)**, the **LTI 1.3 cutover (Dec. 31)** and Ed-Fi API v8.1 set the year-end agenda.
