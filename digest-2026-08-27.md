# EdTech Daily Digest — August 27, 2026

## Executive Summary

EdSurge runs a skeptical follow-up to last week's Department of Education edtech guidance, asking whether a letter from Washington can meaningfully change how **districts and states actually purchase classroom technology** when procurement decisions remain local. A companion EdSurge piece argues educators should stop trying to prevent AI-driven cognitive offloading and instead focus on **preserving the critical-thinking skills** the technology threatens to erode — a theme that echoes Inside Higher Ed's look at colleges adding formal AI-fluency requirements to general-education curricula this fall, and THE Journal's note on the Department of Labor's new AI Literacy Framework. Cybersecurity coverage keeps building on last week's vendor-risk story: EdTech Magazine reports K-12 districts are shifting from reactive incident response to **longer-term cyber resilience strategy**, while its higher-ed edition covers community colleges consolidating security operations centrally, and THE Journal flags a Cloudflare threat report finding hackers increasingly favor **logging in over breaking in**. Higher-ed IT leaders face a tighter budget squeeze: EdTech Magazine covers federal research-funding cuts compounding enrollment pressure, and Inside Higher Ed reports international student visa issuance fell 23% in 2025, a trend NAFSA pegs at $3.4 billion in lost economic activity for the coming academic year. On access, Inside Higher Ed covers Common App's direct-admissions program expanding to 234 colleges reaching first-generation and lower-income students proactively, and 1EdTech holds a webinar today on making verified skills data useful to real-world HR systems.
